\chapter{Obiekty i dane: metody dunder, równość, hashowanie, rekordy}
\label{ch:objects}

\chapterstub{%
\textit{[Opis do przetłumaczenia --- patrz wydanie angielskie.]}

Argument: everything a C\# engineer does with operator overloading,
\code{Equals} and \code{GetHashCode}, \code{record}, \code{IComparable},
\code{ToString}, \code{IDisposable}, properties and indexers is a dunder
method, and the protocol is duck-typed. \code{\_\_eq\_\_} without
\code{\_\_hash\_\_} makes the object unhashable, and that is the chapter's
headline trap; \code{\_\_repr\_\_} against \code{\_\_str\_\_} against
\code{ToString()}; \code{@property} against properties;
\code{\_\_slots\_\_}; a \code{frozen=True} dataclass against a record, with
\code{dataclasses.replace} against \code{with}; \code{Enum}, \code{StrEnum}
and \code{Flag} against an enum; \code{\_\_getitem\_\_}, \code{\_\_len\_\_}
and \code{\_\_iter\_\_} against indexers and \code{IEnumerable}. Class
attributes against instance attributes, and the shared-mutable trap that
follows. Payoff: read any library's class and name the C\# feature it is
imitating. Listings: a Money type written both ways; the \code{\_\_eq\_\_}
and \code{\_\_hash\_\_} breakage reproduced and then fixed. No measurement;
every claim here is about semantics and is checked by a test rather than
timed. Four exercises.
