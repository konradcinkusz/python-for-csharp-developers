\chapter{Funkcje i sterowanie: dekoratory, składanie list, generatory, menedżery kontekstu}
\label{ch:functions}

\chapterstub{%
\textit{[Opis do przetłumaczenia --- patrz wydanie angielskie.]}

Argument: Python's control-flow vocabulary maps onto C\# feature for
feature, and the mapping is where the idioms live. A decorator against an
attribute AND against middleware \dash{} a decorator runs, an attribute is
metadata, and confusing the two is the chapter's headline; \code{*args} and
\code{**kwargs} against \code{params}, optional and named arguments;
keyword-only and positional-only parameters; closures and late binding;
comprehensions against LINQ (eager, where \code{IEnumerable} is deferred
\dash{} and a generator expression IS deferred); generators against
\code{yield return}, and \code{send()}; \code{with} against \code{using},
and \code{contextlib}; \code{match} against switch expressions and pattern
matching; first-class functions against delegates and \code{Func<>};
\code{functools.partial} and \code{lru\_cache}. Payoff: rewrite a LINQ
pipeline as a comprehension and know when to reach for \code{itertools}
instead. Listings: the same pipeline in LINQ and as comprehensions; a
decorator that is middleware, timing and retrying a call. Five exercises.
Overlap rule: the LangChain book's chapter 7 uses decorators for agent
middleware; this chapter owns the mechanism and that one owns the use.
