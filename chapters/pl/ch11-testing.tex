\chapter{Testy: pytest dla tych, którzy znają xUnit, i szkielet trace-assert}
\label{ch:testing}

\chapterstub{%
\textit{[Opis do przetłumaczenia --- patrz wydanie angielskie.]}

Argument: pytest is xUnit with the class removed and the fixture graph made
explicit. Discovery against attributes; a plain \code{assert} with assertion
rewriting against \code{Assert.Equal}; fixtures against a constructor,
\code{IClassFixture} and \code{IAsyncLifetime}, with function, class, module
and session scopes; \code{parametrize} against \code{Theory} and
\code{InlineData}; \code{conftest.py}; \code{monkeypatch} and
\code{unittest.mock} against Moq and NSubstitute, and the trap of patching
where a name is defined rather than where it is looked up; \code{tmp\_path},
\code{caplog} and \code{capsys}; markers and \code{-k}; async tests;
hypothesis against FsCheck; coverage. The trace-assert skeleton: a
\code{Trace} of \code{Event} records, a \code{trace} fixture, the first two
assertions, and the package layout under \code{code/src/trace\_assert/}
\dash{} which is what this book's own CI has been running since chapter 1.
Payoff: a suite that runs with no network, no database server and no model,
and the first stage of the guiding project. Five exercises.
