\chapter{Dane: SQLAlchemy 2.0 dla tych, którzy znają EF Core, i polars dla odruchu LINQ}
\label{ch:data}

\chapterstub{%
\textit{[Opis do przetłumaczenia --- patrz wydanie angielskie.]}

Argument: a SQLAlchemy 2.0 \code{Session} is a unit of work exactly as a
\code{DbContext} is, and the places it differs are the places EF habits
break. Declarative models with \code{Mapped} and \code{mapped\_column}
against entity classes and fluent configuration; \code{select()} against
LINQ to Entities; the identity map, flush against commit,
\code{expire\_on\_commit}; relationships and the N+1 problem (lazy by
default in both, \code{selectinload} against \code{Include}); async sessions
against async EF; transactions and \code{begin()}; Alembic against EF
migrations, where autogenerate is a diff and not a truth; connection
pooling; SQLite for tests against the real engine. polars for the LINQ-over-
a-table reflex: lazy frames against \code{IQueryable}, expressions against
lambdas, and when pandas instead, which for this reader is rarely. Payoff: a
data layer with no repository pattern, and why that pattern is usually a
mistake here, plus a migration workflow. Measurement: experiment E6, the N+1
reproduced and counted from the engine's own echo, before and after
\code{selectinload}, on SQLite, free. Five exercises.
